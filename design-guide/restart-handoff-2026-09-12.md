## User intent that governs interpretation

The user wants a case with the craft of mystery fiction, inventive but checkable mechanisms, meaningful competing explanations, and deep player reasoning. An obscure term or unexplained object is a presentation problem, not difficulty. Preparation, execution, concealment, discovery, mistaken interpretation, and reinterpretation must be connected.

People need motives and work of their own. Extensive dialogue means actual responsive exchanges, not filler, chopped-up short lines, or a late layer of jokes over a minimal puzzle. Entrances, conflicts, requests, experiments, mistakes, discoveries, confrontations, aftermath, and relationship changes need actual scenes.

The clarified eccentricity is **나사 빠진 캐릭터성**. The user rejected the earlier three concept-demo scripts as awkward, including their compressed introductions and forced exchange rhythms. They later preferred the ChatGPT experiment and requested clearer situational grounding, fewer simultaneous new introductions, and shorter repetitive dialogue. The user then accepted that direction. The underlying generated script has not been inspected here.

The child companion preference is retained, but exact identity, age, relationship, and appearance remain to be designed. Neither an omniscient adult voice in a child's body nor a repetitive question device is suitable. Deliberate criminal participation is prohibited in the current case and retrospective sequels.

## Corrections to the proposed production method

The full proposed movable-wall reasoning sequence is one substantial chapter-scale unit. The game needs 5–7 comparably deep, causally connected chapters, each with its own substantial mystery and multiple meaningful deductions. Delaying one mechanism's answer across several thin chapters is not sufficient.

The working method is detailed truth/character/player-reasoning design, non-code structural walkthrough, full Korean script and editorial revision, then adaptation and implementation. Test scenes can calibrate style but cannot replace the full script. A technical slice is an integration check after that preparation, not a reason to constrain the story early.

English is the authoring language for planning. Actual game content and user communication are Korean. ChatGPT is the chosen drafting/editorial workflow based on practical user preference; Codex maintains accepted sources, logic checks, adaptation, and implementation. Do not turn this preference into an untested universal model-performance claim.

The user later clarified that they do not want intermediate reviews. Their only production handoff is sending the prepared sources/prompt to ChatGPT and bringing the returned screenplay files back. Codex owns all other production decisions, editorial checks, revision preparation, acceptance, implementation, and verification. User feedback follows completed-game delivery. This clarification supersedes any earlier suggestion that the user should evaluate every screenplay batch or approve production stages.
