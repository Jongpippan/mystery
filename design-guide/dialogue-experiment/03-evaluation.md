## Procedure

1. Keep first drafts and revisions separately. Compare first draft with first draft, then revision with revision.
2. Assign A/B labels without visible product names when practical. Do not edit the text to make one candidate look better.
3. Read each screenplay without its author notes. Assess comprehension before checking source compliance.
4. Write down particular lines or transitions that worked or failed. A general statement such as "more natural" is insufficient evidence by itself.
5. Check the source afterward for continuity and invention. A charming scene can still violate its brief.
6. Reveal the workflow labels only after recording your preference, if blinding was possible.

The user helped shape the fixture and cannot become an uninformed reader by hiding the source for a moment. Record that limitation. A separate reader who has never seen the planning notes can test first-encounter comprehension more independently.

## Reader questions — before consulting character notes

- Why have 서진 and 봄 come to the hotel, and why is the child with that adult?

- Where did you lose track of an action, speaker intention, topic change, or physical position?

Do not require the screenplay to disclose every fact in the source. Understandable introductions and an actual encounter matter more than biographical completeness.

## Interpretation

A single pilot does not prove general superiority of ChatGPT, Codex, English planning, a model family, or a reasoning setting. Different models or surrounding instructions mean this is a practical workflow comparison, not an isolated interface experiment.
