# KDW-01 — Source for a Korean introductory scene

Version: 1.0 / 2026-09-12
Language: English author guidance; Korean screenplay output.
Status: provisional writing experiment, not final game canon.

## 1. Assignment context

Write an original Korean scene for an adult-audience mystery game with an eccentric child companion. The work should eventually support a substantial, intricately plotted mystery and a large actual script. In the full-game requirement, the complete movable-wall investigation is roughly one chapter's worth of reasoning; the game needs 5–7 substantial chapters of comparable depth connected into one developing story. The full murder case and those chapters have not yet been designed. Do not invent them in this exercise.

This task tests an introduction before any crime has been discovered. It contains no deduction submission, branching interface, culprit revelation, or final mystery explanation. Its modest local objective is deliberately chosen to isolate dialogue and characterization; it does not reduce the game's intended scale.

The reader starts with no character biography, hotel knowledge, or prior scene. The characters have histories, but the reader must meet them through what happens now.

## 2. Intended reading experience

The reader should understand the ordinary situation without struggling: why the visitors have come, why the hotel is closing, why someone in a doorman's uniform is being treated as a guest, who the child is with, and what the group is trying to accomplish.

The people should feel conspicuously eccentric, sometimes foolish, and capable of being funny without recognizing that they are funny. The user's intended flavor is **나사 빠진 캐릭터성**. This is stronger than quiet charm, polished banter, or one whimsical hobby per person. Their skewed priorities should affect their choices and the scene.

Allow them ordinary speech and unremarkable actions as well. Characters do not need to defend every oddity with a sensible philosophy, a tragic backstory, or an explanation of their comedic function. They also do not become randomly incoherent. The same person should remain recognizable from one action to the next.

Humor is an ambition, not a quota. Reactions can be distracted, practical, indulgent, embarrassed, annoyed, or absent. A strange act does not require a partner to ask the exact question that exposes its joke. The child is not a miniature adult stand-up comedian. The protagonist is not merely a straight man who supplies prompts for everyone else's punchlines.

## 3. Local facts — fixed for this test only

| Fact | Fixture value |
|---|---|
| Hotel | 해명호텔, an aging independent hotel still functioning on its last day |
| Date and time | Early autumn, 15:20; no exact calendar date is necessary |
| Closure | A sale has been agreed. Guest service ends at 22:00 tonight. Packing is under way, but guests and staff still have legitimate reasons to be here. |

| Later event | A small farewell gathering is planned for the evening. Its room layout and program are outside this scene. |
| Available space | The entrance, reception counter, luggage trolley, lobby seating, and an open area suitable for a group photograph |
| Visual state | Some empty boxes, packing tape, a partly cleared brochure rack, and ordinary work still in progress. The hotel is not an abandoned ruin. |

No one is trapped. No body has been found. There is no ominous scream, blackout, threatening call, anonymous message, or newly invented crime clue at the end.

## 4. Cast — author knowledge, not reader-facing introductions

The names and relationship below are experimental choices. Exact age, appearance, gender presentation, long-term companion relationship, and protagonist's later investigative authority are not established by this fixture. Do not add precise ages or a future detective backstory.

### P01 — 윤서진, protagonist

**Current reason to be here:** A freelance photographer hired to record the hotel's final day. 서진 has photographed staff here before, knows 문식 and 해경, and has agreed to supervise 봄 while 봄's mother finishes her shift. The parent and 서진 are long-standing friends; 봄 knows 서진 well and calls them `서진`, without a gendered family title.

**Immediate objective:** Put down the equipment, locate the person coordinating the photograph, and get enough cooperation to start on time. 서진 also needs to keep track of a tired child without turning the entire visit into a reprimand.

**Personality:** Outwardly capable, socially a little presumptuous, and absurdly persistent about small photographic details. 서진 will rearrange a stranger's bag before remembering to ask. When trying to include a difficult person, 서진 sometimes promises a photographic favor without thinking through the work it creates. This is a source of active behavior, not a requirement for a running joke about cameras.

**Speech and relationships:** Conversational Korean, polite to the older staff, familiar with 봄. Can be amused or impatient, can misunderstand, and sometimes makes the practical problem worse. No constant detective aphorisms, rhetorical deductions, or crisp sarcastic finales. 서진 knows the staff well enough to be received warmly but does not know their private thoughts.

### P02 — 최봄, child companion

**Current reason to be here:** 봄's mother works in the hotel kitchen. 서진 is the agreed adult supervisor until the mother's shift ends. The mother remains offstage and reachable; no additional speaking character is needed.

**Immediate objective:** Help take the photograph and get a turn with the camera, rather than be deposited on a lobby chair. 봄 has brought a small bag and a pillow because a short afternoon rest was arranged. 봄 now regards sleeping through any of the last day's hotel service as wasting the visit.

**Personality:** Overcommits to an exciting plan and then conceals small, obvious failures. 봄 tried to get extra sleep the day before to avoid needing a nap today and is annoyed that the plan has failed. The character can persist in an obviously defective solution, change the subject to save face, or take a promise more literally than intended. This background is available to motivate behavior; it must not all be recited as an explanatory speech.

**Speech and relationships:** Familiar speech to 서진, mostly polite to the staff, with lapses when sleepy or absorbed. Vocabulary and sentence rhythms should sound like a child, without baby-talk spelling or adult comic precision. 봄 already knows 해경 through the mother's work, but knows 문식 only as the person who opens the entrance door. Their new guest arrangement is unknown to 봄.

**Competence and limits:** Can help with a small, safe photography task under supervision and notice someone's immediate needs. Does not solve a mystery, handle dangerous equipment, roam unsupervised into service areas, or secretly participate in a crime. Do not turn every observation into a future clue.

### P03 — 장문식, former head doorman

**Current situation:** Retired yesterday after many years at this hotel. Paid for a day-use room today because he wants, just once, to experience the service he used to provide. He has already checked in. He still wears the uniform: the staff photograph is today, and he takes that photograph seriously.

**Immediate objective:** Get someone else to carry his suitcase upstairs while remaining available for the photograph. The suitcase contains ordinary personal things and is not a suspicious container. He is not pretending to be retired, and he has not stolen a room.

**Personality:** Has overprepared for being an unreasonable hotel guest, including writing down petty requests, but cannot tolerate seeing the work done badly. He may unconsciously perform the service he meant to demand, then try to reclaim his guest status. He sincerely wants the experience and can be childish about being denied it. He is skilled at hospitality; retirement does not make him confused or incapable.

**Speech and relationships:** Older, direct, accustomed to giving practical instructions. Polite formality may surface when performing the role of a guest, but it must not become the same catchphrase in every turn. He and 해경 have worked together for years. Their familiarity can be affectionate and irritating without converting them into people who trade perfectly balanced jokes.

**Emotional range:** Wants to be included in the staff photograph even while insisting he is a customer. He need not confess that feeling. Do not force a tearful speech about lifelong service into the opening.

### P04 — 서해경, operations manager

**Current reason to be here:** Coordinating the final day's work and photograph while continuing to serve guests. She needs 문식's knowledge and help, but his retirement is real; she cannot simply order him back onto payroll.

**Immediate objective:** Bring the photographer and the available staff into position without delaying ongoing work. She enters from reception after handling a mundane guest request, not to announce her personality.

**Personality:** Has an extravagantly inflated idea of what the farewell deserves. She has arranged a much too large commemorative ribbon for a small gathering and remains disproportionately proud of it. The ribbon may stay offstage; it is not a mandatory gag. In ordinary work she improvises shabbily while demanding ceremonial grandeur later. She can negotiate a ridiculous compromise with complete sincerity.

**Speech and relationships:** Busy, colloquial, and capable of longer requests that change direction midway. She expects 문식 to know what she means and sometimes starts asking him for help before remembering the new situation. She knows 서진's work and 봄's mother. She does not explain the staff's shared history for the audience in a single speech.

**Emotional range:** Her concern about the final photograph is real, but not every ridiculous act needs to be redeemed by a touching explanation. Give her a response and a want of her own, not only the task of correcting 문식.

## 5. Scene S01 — Arrival and getting ready for the photograph

### Starting state

서진 and 봄 enter together at 15:20. 서진 carries a camera bag and folded tripod; 봄 has the small bag and pillow. 문식 is near the luggage trolley with his suitcase. 해경 is initially occupied at reception and becomes involved because these people's attempts to get started affect her work.

The reader has not met anyone. Establish the hotel as a working place in the process of closing through the encounter. Introduce identities and forms of address when a person would actually use them. A cast list before the scene is not allowed.

### Dramatic work

- Make the arrival and photographic assignment comprehensible.
- Let the reader discover why the uniformed man is behaving as a guest.
- Establish why the child is with 서진, without interrupting the scene for a guardian-history briefing.
- Develop the collision between 문식's desired treatment and 해경's need for cooperation. Give both room to act and respond.
- Let 서진 and 봄 affect this collision instead of merely comment on it.
- Arrive at a small, workable agreement and begin arranging the photograph. Leave room for ordinary business, movement, pauses, and incomplete emotional expression.

These are outcomes, not a prescribed sequence of jokes or individual beats. You may choose which eccentricities appear now. Do not try to demonstrate every trait in the source. One behavior can develop over several exchanges; personalities should emerge cumulatively.

### Scope and length

Write one continuous scene of roughly **3,500–5,000 Korean characters including spaces**, counting dialogue and action directions. This is a comparison target, not a demand for padding or an exact-count claim. If space is tight, prioritize a complete encounter over a hurried parade of gags.

Use a short Korean scene heading, speaker labels, actual dialogue, and readable Korean action directions. Actions must show what can be seen or heard; do not insert author explanations of who is eccentric or why something is funny. Use varied sentence and turn lengths. No code, JSON, synopsis, numbered beat outline, quality self-rating, or reader-facing personality notes.

For this first test, do not include choice branches or UI markers. This keeps the comparison focused; the eventual game still requires actual player choices, responses, retained answers, and rich branching dialogue.

### Allowed invention

Choose natural wording, gestures, blocking within the lobby, connective conversation, and ordinary small props. You can invent minor incidental details that do not affect a mystery, alter established relationships, or predetermine later plot. The source describes motives, not mandatory lines.

### Boundaries

Do not invent a culprit, victim, secret relationship, crime method, exact death time, locked-room condition, incriminating document, or full chapter outline. Do not reveal or explicitly foreshadow the movable-wall mechanism, discuss plans or wall measurements, or send the scene into a room whose geometry matters. This is a local source boundary for a pre-incident test, not a license to conceal available evidence in the eventual investigation.

No character has knowledge of a future crime. There is no compulsory ominous ending. The helper child never intentionally assists crime. Serious harm is not a punchline.

## 6. Editorial targets

The previous attempted samples failed because they were compressed personality demonstrations with mechanical setup-and-punchline exchanges. They are not supplied, and their wording should not be reconstructed.

The correction is a fully developed encounter: people arrive for reasons, try to do something, misjudge each other, react to what actually happened, and reach a changed practical situation. Natural dialogue can be loose, unfinished, or quite plain. Preserve strong absurdity in the people; do not solve awkward writing by making them all restrained, reasonable, and interchangeable.

The final test is the screenplay without this source beside it. Can a new reader follow the situation, recognize different people, and want to stay with them?
